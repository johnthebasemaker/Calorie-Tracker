Privacy Policy

Calorie Tracker (com.john3004.calorietracker) · Effective 21 September 2026

This app does not collect any of your data. There is no account, no sign-in, no server belonging to this app, no analytics and no advertising. Everything you log stays in storage on your own device.

What the app stores, and where

Your food log, custom foods, water, workouts, body details and settings are saved in your device's local browser storage, inside the app's own private storage area. This data:

  • is never uploaded to any server run by this app, because there is none;
  • is not readable by other apps;
  • is excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup, deliberately — see below;
  • is deleted completely when you uninstall the app.

What leaves your device

The app works fully offline. Three optional features make requests to outside services, and nothing else does. No request carries your name, your email, an account id or an advertising id, because the app holds none of them.

OpenRouter Only if you turn on AI summary and paste in your own API key See below — this one is the most sensitive

Location

The app never asks for or uses your device's location. The city used for the weather adjustment is one you type in yourself, and you can leave it blank.

The AI feature, in detail

AI summary is off by default and cannot run until you paste in your own OpenRouter API key. If you turn it on, requests to OpenRouter may include:

  • the foods you logged that day, their portions and the times you ate them;
  • your calorie and nutrient totals and targets;
  • the names of foods in your library, so suggestions match what you eat;
  • if you filled in My Profile: your age, sex, height, weight and goal — this is health-related information about you;
  • any free-text goal you wrote yourself.

Your use of that feature is governed by OpenRouter's privacy policy and by the policy of whichever model provider serves the request. If you would rather share none of this, leave AI summary switched off, or clear the API key in Settings. The rest of the app is unaffected.

Your API key is stored only on your device. It is sent to OpenRouter to authenticate your own requests and nowhere else, and it is deliberately left out of the app's own backup export.

Camera

The app asks for camera permission for one purpose: reading a barcode off a packet. The camera image is decoded to a number inside the app and discarded immediately. No photo or video is saved, kept, or transmitted anywhere. Only the decoded barcode number is sent, to Open Food Facts, to look up the product.

You can refuse the permission and still use the whole app — there is a field for typing the barcode number by hand.

Backups

Android's automatic cloud backup is switched off for this app on purpose. Had it been left on, your log would have been copied to Google's servers — and so would your OpenRouter API key, which is stored alongside it.

Instead, Settings has an Export that writes a backup file you control, and it deliberately leaves the API key out. Where you then put that file is up to you.

Your control over the data

  • See it: Settings → Export writes everything out as a readable JSON file.
  • Delete it: uninstalling the app removes all of it. There is nothing held elsewhere to request the deletion of, because nothing was ever sent.
  • Stop the outbound requests: turn off AI summary, clear the city, and avoid barcode search. The app still works offline.

Children

This app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this same address with a new effective date. The page's full history is public in the repository, so any change can be inspected.
